Sulvo: Contour Water Puzzle (“Sulvo”, “the game”) is a puzzle played on a survey map: you move soil from one spot to another, then release the water and watch where it flows. The developer is Pairo (“we”, “us”). This policy explains what the game handles on Android and on iPhone and iPad, for what purpose, who else receives it, how long it is kept and what you can decide.

The short version

  • Sulvo has no account. It never asks for or reads your name, e-mail address, phone number, contacts, photos, camera, microphone or location.
  • Your levels, stars, coins, atlas pages and settings stay on your device. We do not run a server that stores them.
  • A random player number, some device facts, usage events and crash reports go to Google Firebase so we can repair and improve the game.
  • Ads come from Google AdMob. On iPhone and iPad the advertising ID is used only if you allow tracking; where a consent form applies (for example in Europe and the UK) ads wait until you have chosen.
  • Payments go through Apple or Google, and RevenueCat confirms them. We never see your card details.
  • For any request, write to the address at the bottom of this page.

What the game handles

Player number and device details

On the first launch Sulvo makes up a random player number of 8 characters. It has nothing to do with your name, phone number or the serial number of your device. The number is attached to what is sent to Firebase (Analytics and Crashlytics) together with the device model, system version, language, app version and the amount of memory, and RevenueCat uses it as your anonymous customer ID. You can read and copy it in Settings.

Usage events (Firebase Analytics)

The game notes short events: that it was opened, a level was started or finished, how many stars you got and how much soil you moved, whether a hint or a survey was used, visits to the daily map, the endless map and the atlas, screens and language changes, plus ad and purchase events. They show us which levels are too hard, where players give up and whether a change helped. No event contains typed text, because Sulvo has no text fields. The daily map is chosen from the date on your device; the game never reads your location for it.

Crash reports (Crashlytics)

When the game crashes or meets an error, Crashlytics receives a report: the technical trace, the condition of the device, a short trail of your latest actions (such as screens opened or ads shown), the app version, the language, whether ads are removed, the current screen and the player number. We use it only to find and fix the fault.

Game settings from the network (Firebase Remote Config)

On start the game fetches a few values from Firebase: the oldest and the newest supported version, how often an ad may appear and the coin amounts of some rewards and prices. Firebase uses an installation ID of the app and the usual connection data to answer.

Ads (Google AdMob)

Sulvo shows a banner on the level screen, an ad when you leave a result screen and optional rewarded ads. To deliver and measure them Google receives your advertising ID (on iPhone and iPad only with your tracking permission; on Android through the AD_ID permission), your IP address and the rough location derived from it, device and software details and how you react to ads. Google may use this to personalise ads, to limit how often one appears and to prevent fraud.

Purchases (RevenueCat, App Store, Google Play)

Coin packs and Remove Ads are purchased from the App Store or Google Play. RevenueCat receives the receipt, the product, your player number, your country and IP address so that it can confirm the purchase and remember whether Remove Ads is active. Card and bank details stay with Apple or Google.

Connection check and rating prompt

To learn whether you are online, the game requests an empty answer from a Google connectivity address; no game data goes with the request. After some levels the game may open the rating window of Apple or Google; what you enter there is handled by the store and is not passed on to us.

What stays on your device

Progress, stars, coins, atlas pages, the daily streak, settings and your consent choice are kept in the game’s storage on your device and are not sent to us. Uninstalling the game deletes them, and they do not move to a new device.

What we use it for

  • Running the game: confirming purchases, switching ads off after Remove Ads, applying the right version rules.
  • Understanding and improving it: finding crashes, tuning the difficulty of levels, learning which features people use.
  • Showing ads, which pay for the free game, and measuring and limiting them.
  • Protecting the game and its ads from fraud and misuse.

We do not sell your data for money and we do not assemble profiles of you ourselves.

Consent and your choices

  • Europe and the UK: before ads are requested, Google’s consent form (UMP) asks you to accept or refuse. You can open it again in Settings › Privacy options.
  • iPhone and iPad: the system asks whether Sulvo may track you. If you refuse, the advertising ID is not used and ads are not personalised. You can change it in Settings › Privacy & Security › Tracking on your device.
  • Android: reset or delete your advertising ID, and turn off ad personalisation, in your device’s Settings › Google › Ads.
  • Rewarded ads are always your choice. Every level can be played without watching any ad, and Remove Ads switches off the banner and the ads between levels.

How long it is kept

  • Firebase Analytics events: kept for at most 14 months.
  • Crashlytics crash reports: roughly 90 days.
  • Google AdMob: under Google’s own retention rules for advertising data.
  • RevenueCat purchase records: for as long as they are needed to confirm purchases, or until you ask for deletion.
  • On your device: until the game is uninstalled.

Who receives it and where it goes

Google (Firebase and AdMob) and RevenueCat are the recipients of the data described above. Firebase and RevenueCat work for us as service providers; Google acts for advertising under its own terms. Apple and Google run the stores and the payments. These companies may process data in the United States and in other countries; where the law asks for it, transfers rest on safeguards such as standard contractual clauses. We pass data to no one else.

Your rights

Since there is no account, we can find your data only through your player number. Write to us with the number shown in Settings and say what you want: a copy of the data tied to it, a correction or its deletion. We reply within 30 days. Depending on where you live you may also object to or limit processing, withdraw consent and complain to your data protection authority.

Legal bases and regional notes

  • EU, EEA and UK (GDPR): ads and the advertising ID rest on your consent; usage events and crash reports rest on our legitimate interest in keeping the game running and making it better; purchases rest on the contract you make with the store.
  • California and other US states: we never sell personal data for money. Giving Google the advertising ID for advertising may count as “sharing” or “sale” under some state laws; you can opt out in Settings › Privacy options, in your device’s ad settings or by writing to us. You will not be treated worse for doing so.
  • Elsewhere: we follow the rules of the place where you live, as far as they apply to a game without accounts.

Children

Sulvo is made for players aged 13 and older and is not aimed at children. We do not knowingly gather data from anyone under 13. If you think a child has played the game, write to us and we will delete whatever we can find.

Security

Everything sent to Google and RevenueCat is carried over encrypted connections. We keep no account database, so we hold very little that could be lost; Google and RevenueCat protect their own copies with their own safeguards. No system is perfectly safe, which is why the data we handle is kept to the minimum described here.

Changes

If the game begins to handle data in a new way, for example through a new service, we update this page before the change reaches you and change the effective date above. Small wording fixes may be made without notice.
